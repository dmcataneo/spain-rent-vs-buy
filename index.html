How it works

Hey! After building the mortgage simulator I wanted to answer the question people actually ask first: is it even worth buying where I live? The honest answer changes a lot from one province to the next, so this uses official data for each one.

You and a twin start with the same cash. The buyer puts it into a home with a mortgage. The renter rents the same kind of home and invests that cash. Every month, whoever pays less than the other invests the difference. The chart shows what each of you would have if you sold or stopped renting after each year, and the break-even is the first year the buyer is ahead.

It's a small experiment, not financial advice. The assumptions (growth, returns, costs) are yours to change, and the answer moves a lot when you do.

For the mortgage side in detail (fixed vs variable, early repayment, Euribor history), see the Spain mortgage simulator.

Where the numbers come from.

  • Rent: average monthly rent of main-home leases, by province, from the tax agency's IRPF housing statistics, tax year 2024 (AEAT). These are declared leases, so they include older contracts and usually sit below what a new listing asks.
  • Price: average appraised value per m2 by province, from the Ministry of Housing's appraisal statistic, read through the ISTAC open data copy (dataset). Appraisals are not sale prices. You can choose the 2024 average (same year as the rent) or the newest quarter, 2026 Q2.
  • Size: the average size of rented homes in each province, from the same tax table. You can scale it.

Limits. The tax statistic does not cover Araba/Álava, Bizkaia, Gipuzkoa or Navarra, so they are not here, and no national average is used in their place. Rent is one average per province, not per town or neighbourhood. Buying costs and taxes vary by region, so they are one input. No mortgage subsidies, no tax relief, no rent control, no vacancy.
